# Product value and offer model

Use for a new product model, a changed mechanism, or a material offer decision.
Start from the current brief and evidence; an idea supports a hypothesis, not
validated demand. Preserve consumer, service, internal-tool, cashflow, and
venture objectives rather than forcing a subscription or platform.

## Outcome and mechanism

Identify beneficiary, user, buyer and payer; these may be the same person.
State the job, current workaround, starting state, desired transition, horizon,
and observable outcome. Classify the need as finite, episodic, recurring, or
capability development. Successful completion or independence can reduce use.

Compare deterministic software, a human/service process, adaptive AI, and a
hybrid against the same population and outcome. Include active effort, elapsed
time, reliability, correction, support, variable cost, and authority. Honor an
explicitly chosen agent scope while testing its incremental contribution
against a simpler baseline. Deep agent mechanics belong to
cascade-ai-architect:design-agent-blueprint.

Choose a thin slice that closes one useful transition: trigger -> useful help
-> action -> observed outcome -> appropriate completion or next action.
Distinguish time to feedback from time to improvement. Add organizational or
learning loops only when a named beneficiary uses the result for a decision.
Data collection is a cost unless that decision justifies purpose, access,
correction, retention, and operational burden.

## Exchange and adoption

For pricing or packaging, compare one-time, episodic, usage, service, program,
and subscription models against the actual job. Define a value metric that
reflects the benefit without unpredictable cost or manufactured recurrence.
Keep price and willingness-to-pay hypotheses separate from observed payment.

State the offer's audience, promised transition, mechanism, proof, conditions,
time/effort expectation, price, limits, and exit/refund terms. Trace expectation
through first useful action, realized outcome, and appropriate renewal or exit.
Identify procurement, implementation, training, integrations, and support when
they affect adoption; a signed contract does not prove adoption or value.

Expose who benefits, does additional work, pays, bears risk, controls data, and
can correct or refuse. Do not hide stakeholder losses inside an aggregate score.
Include refunds, unpaid usage, sales effort, human review, delivery and support
in net economics. Missing costs remain unknown rather than zero.

## Product contract binding

Use the existing PRODUCT_DEFINITION sections, journeys, requirements, and
validation_claims. Give value-model and offer sections stable section_id values;
link feature requirements to those sections through traceability and bind
external sources through evidence_ids. Never add a second strategy database or
undeclared fields to the Product schema.

Record chosen and rejected mechanisms, non-goals, strongest contrary evidence,
decisive unknown, and the smallest useful test. Keep assumptions explicit and
preserve the existing completeness and approval statuses. User intent is valid
evidence of intent; it is not market or efficacy proof.
