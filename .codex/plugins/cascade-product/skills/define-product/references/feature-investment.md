# Feature formation and investment

Use when generating, comparing, splitting, prioritizing, or retiring features.
For implementation of an already accepted mechanical change, use the host
implementation route without reopening market strategy.

## Form candidates

Derive candidates from an unmet outcome, a failed workflow transition,
observed friction, missing proof, or an accepted differentiation hypothesis.
Competitor presence and availability of AI are context, not sufficient reasons
to build. Compare a feature with a smaller workflow change, manual delivery,
revised offer/message, and deliberate non-build.

For each material candidate preserve:

- actor, trigger, job, workaround, and evidence of need;
- outcome and causal mechanism: use -> changed action/state -> useful outcome;
- supporting evidence, counterevidence, assumptions, and alternative explanations;
- minimum vertical slice, what stays manual, and what is omitted;
- dependencies, system of record, interfaces, failure/recovery and rollout;
- effort, variable cost, staff review, adoption and interaction burden;
- activation, offer, payment, appropriate return/completion, and referral effects;
- success and guardrail measures, failure/inconclusive actions, and owner.

Compare candidates on the same outcome, population, horizon, and constraints.
Do not sum incomparable stakeholder benefits into a confident score. Failed
trust, authority, feasibility, or economic conditions cannot be averaged away.
Prefer a complete useful loop to several disconnected capabilities.

Decide build, prototype, test demand, test mechanism, revise offer, research,
split, defer, or reject. A proposed build is not implementation authorization.
Portfolio allocation across different products belongs to
cascade-product:manage-product-lifecycle.

## Bind the feature to delivery

Keep the existing Product artifact as the source of accepted behavior. Represent
the candidate rationale in a section; accepted features become requirement_id
entries with positive, negative, failure/recovery and measurable acceptance
behavior where applicable. Trace each requirement to its outcome/value section
and source evidence. Keep unresolved candidates and rejected alternatives in
sections rather than disguising them as accepted requirements.

The handoff must make the intended behavior, outcome, non-goals, dependency,
proof, rollout, and stop conditions visible to development. Mark proposed
numeric thresholds as awaiting owner acceptance, not facts derived from pain
evidence. Route interaction uncertainty to cascade-design:ux-flow-review and
measurement uncertainty to cascade-product:validate-product.

Growth evidence can challenge a message, audience, offer, experience, mechanism,
or feature. Diagnose which assumption failed before adding a feature. Preserve
the original growth artifact identity and affected requirement/section IDs;
lifecycle review reopens only the earliest challenged decision.
