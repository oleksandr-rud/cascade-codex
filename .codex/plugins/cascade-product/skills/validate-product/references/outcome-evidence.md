# Outcome and progress evidence

Use when evaluating progress, product efficacy, or claims about improvement.
Define the construct, actor, decision, horizon and intended use of the claim.

Separate exposure, activity, completed action, immediate change, sustained
change, transfer to a relevant unassisted setting, and incremental effect.
Not every job requires every layer. A useful completed finite job can be
success without retention; an unknown outcome is not successful graduation.

Bind baseline, instrument/version, population, observation window, conditions,
comparison, uncertainty and missingness. A qualitative criterion is valid when
observable; do not invent sample counts, effect sizes or numeric confidence.
Check whether the measure changed alongside the product, whether the same
people remained in the cohort, and whether conditions are comparable.

Self-report supports reported experience. Repeated coached tasks do not prove
unassisted transfer. Coaching and grading by the same model need independent
calibration before stronger efficacy claims. A before/after improvement alone
does not identify the product's incremental effect: inspect selection, practice,
other interventions and alternative explanations.

Invalid measurement makes an efficacy test inconclusive; it does not establish
that the intervention failed. State the supported claim, unsupported stronger
claim, uncertainty, allowed next decision, next evidence and owner. Keep
intervention effects distinct from simple temporal association.

Represent measures and results in the existing validation_claims, sections and
evidence bindings. Link affected requirements and their decision IDs, and
return learning to cascade-product:manage-product-lifecycle. Marketing may
consume the supported claim with its population, product version and horizon;
it must not promote it to a stronger promise. Personal feedback or reusable
guidance needs provenance, applicability limits, correction and expiry.
