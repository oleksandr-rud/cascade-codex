# Entry strategy and differentiation

Evaluate a narrow reason to choose alongside the existing frozen opportunity
dimensions and hard gates. Do not replace the typed scoring contract or let a
high average override an evidence gap, failed floor or disqualifier.

Compare the same actor, problem, context, desired outcome and alternatives.
List important category assumptions about workflow, buyer, pricing, technology,
distribution, and recurrence. Look for a conflict between purchase promises
and realized value, or between requested features and actual outcomes.

Propose an evidence-linked reason to choose, its product consequence, strongest
counterevidence, and falsifier. Novelty is not a universal requirement.
Cashflow, service and internal-efficiency goals can use a credible access,
localization, usability, reliability or cost advantage. Venture ambitions may
need a more durable non-consensus thesis and plausible adjacent expansion.

When a contrarian thesis is relevant: the category assumes X; the candidate
hypothesis is Y; evidence/causal reasoning is Z; this enables action A; result
B would refute it. Opposition alone is not evidence. Check substitutes and
current competitors before calling a space neglected.

Assess imitation, incumbent response, distribution concentration, useful
switching advantages and customer exit. Prompts, generic AI access, feature
count and a single early ad winner do not establish defensibility. Avoid
coercive lock-in and decorative differentiation with no outcome effect.

Keep an uncertain entry strategy as a hypothesis in the opportunity rationale,
with evidence IDs and next experiment. Positioning language belongs to
cascade-market:brand-positioning; product mechanisms and feature acceptance
belong to cascade-product:define-product. Market assessment remains a
recommendation, not product approval.
