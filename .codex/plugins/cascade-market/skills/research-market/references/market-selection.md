# Market discovery and comparable evidence

Choose explore, shortlist, validate, or commit from the user's current decision.
Preserve positive constraints, exclusions, geography, objective and horizon.
Do not narrow an open search to a historical example or the founder's background.

Generate options across distinct jobs. For each, compare actor, job, current
workaround, natural frequency, urgency/consequence, buyer/payer, willingness to
pay, alternatives, distribution access, cost structure, constraints and the
next uncertainty. Record search coverage and sources so unequal investigation
does not become a fabricated market ranking.

Inspect how attention and desire spread: urgent events, intentional search,
trusted exemplars, professional context, identity or status. Distinguish a
durable useful job from fashion or rivalry. A desired outcome should remain
meaningful without a named rival or rank; do not manufacture insecurity.

Use current primary evidence where available: observed workarounds, payment,
switching, product/pricing pages, official catalogs, public ad libraries, job
requests, and attributable reviews. Interviews should concern actual past
behavior, circumstances, costs and workarounds before opinions about the idea.
Record recruitment, question framing, contrary cases and selection limits.
There is no universal interview or signup count that establishes validation.

Competitor revenue, ads, rankings and reviews support limited category claims.
They do not prove entrant acquisition cost, retention or margin. Include failed
entrants, inactive products, spend and selection bias where available. Treat
self-reported revenue and provider estimates as such; missing denominators
remain limitations.

Compare reach through paid, search, content, community, partners, sales and
platforms where relevant. Distinguish discoverable metadata from actual tool
access or a successful query. Do not claim an integration, scrape, interview,
or live test ran from its plan. The existing evidence ledger owns source IDs,
dates, contradictions, evidence classes and permitted uses.

Use cascade-market:evaluate-market-opportunity to compare the frozen evidence.
If evidence does not justify a winner, recommend the cheapest differentiating
test or a shortlist. A category can be attractive while the entrant remains
unvalidated.
