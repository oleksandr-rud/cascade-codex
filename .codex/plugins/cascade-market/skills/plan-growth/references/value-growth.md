# Growth from promise to outcome

Express the promise as a useful transition for a named actor under explicit
conditions. Connect promise, expectation, first useful action, observed outcome,
payment, appropriate return/completion and sustainable exchange. Activation
must indicate meaningful readiness or action, not just finishing onboarding.

Keep the destination outcome-focused: useful information, a clear next action,
and honest result/recovery states. Diagnose friction in that transition rather
than replacing it with a story about system activity. Product defines changed
behavior; Cascade Design owns the shared UI default. Visual polish and reported
activity cannot stand in for activation, useful outcomes, or payment evidence.

Preserve audience, channel, creative, offer, product version, starting context
and cohort when they affect interpretation. Separate exposure denominators
from qualified users and from each later transition. Failed or abandoned
cohorts belong in the economics and evidence limitations.

## Concepts and lifecycle

Compare distinct concepts: recognizing an urgent job, a useful demonstration,
practical teaching, reduced effort, progress evidence, a trusted context, or a
completed-result story. Hook edits alone are correlated variants. Check each
concept's promise, visible mechanism, inspectable proof, destination, activation
experience and trust boundary.

Use supported product language and observable outcomes. A testimonial supports
that reported experience; a demonstration supports that capability; a causal
claim needs suitable outcome evidence. Track which wording each source permits.
Do not invent scarcity, customer results or insecurity to improve conversion.

Match the lifecycle to the job. Classify completion, graduation, episodic or
seasonal return, abandonment and unknown outcomes before designing retention
interventions. A finite job may succeed with no return visit; recurrent needs
require evidence beyond first-session novelty.

## Economics, learning and product feedback

Connect acquisition to net proceeds, refunds, variable delivery and support,
payment and observed outcomes. Do not scale from gross revenue, low historical
acquisition cost, unobserved retention, or an assumed lifetime-value tail.
Inspect marginal cost, saturation and concentration as scale changes.

Route a failed promise/proof to positioning, an exchange problem to Product's
offer model, interaction burden to Design through Product, and a mechanism or
feature problem to Product. Outcome-measurement failure makes an efficacy test
inconclusive. Successful acquisition does not validate a feature's causal effect.

Carry evidence and versioned Product requirement IDs to the earliest challenged
decision. Reopen affected dependent work through Product lifecycle management;
do not keep a contradicted roadmap active or rerun unrelated discovery.
