# Research and information architecture

Run this phase when evidence, user needs or structure materially changes the
design decision. Reuse the current intake record. Do not require discovery or
participant studies for a demonstrated local correction.

## Frame the uncertainty

State the decision to be made, current evidence, the unresolved hypothesis and
what observation would change the design. Distinguish commissioned behavior,
external market facts, actual interface observations, participant evidence and
designer assumptions. Product/Market owns its behavioral/market decisions;
Design can plan research and interpret supplied evidence within those bounds.

Choose the smallest suitable instrument: source review, competitor experience
inspection, task observation, usability test, interview, card sort, tree test,
analytics review or a bounded prototype experiment. Name the participant segment,
context and sampling limits when real people are involved. The host owns access,
recruitment, communications, privacy and operational authorization. No copied
template authorizes sending invitations, recording people or exporting data.

## Make a usable instrument

Write neutral tasks from the actor's job. Avoid leading people toward the
preferred feature or asking whether they like a proposed solution as proof of
utility. Start with context/current behavior; then observe a concrete task,
decisions, breakdowns, recovery and completion. Separate what was said from what
was observed. Capture consent/access limits through the owning host process.

For a usability session, define task, initial state, success/failure signal,
moderator prompts and recording method before execution. Do not explain the UI
unless that intervention is itself recorded. For a survey or interview, retain
the actual question wording and respondent context. Use local research and
usability references from the vendor index for full instrument patterns.

Synthetic personas and actor simulations can rehearse questions or expose
possible gaps. Label them synthetic, with the fixed scenario and limits. They
cannot establish participant prevalence, behavior, conversion or usability.
Requested bounded simulation goes to its execution owner; it is not run inside
UX or visual review.

## Synthesize without inflating evidence

Create one evidence ledger with source ID, date/revision, population/context,
observation or quote, interpretation, consequence, uncertainty and decision link.
Deduplicate repeated reports of the same event. Preserve contradictory and
negative evidence instead of silently averaging it away. A theme names the
evidence supporting it; frequency and sample size are actual counts, never model
confidence disguised as research statistics.

Group by actor/job, trigger, desired progress, constraint, breakdown and recovery.
Make distinctions that affect the interface: novice/expert context, device,
permission, interruptions, information needs or operational risk. Do not turn
thematic names into ungrounded universal personas. State what remains unknown
and which decision it blocks. External facts need traceable current sources;
local source examples are hypotheses, not evidence about the target market.

## Build the structure

1. Inventory actual entities, content, tasks and existing navigation. Record
   required labels, units, relationships and ownership. A page inventory alone
   is not a user journey.
2. Map the actor's entry, decision points, actions, carried state, interruption,
   return and useful completion. Separate information architecture from workflow
   ordering; navigation can support several valid paths.
3. Group content by how the actor retrieves/compares/acts. Use plain labels and
   meaningful hierarchy. Preserve necessary comparison fields; do not hide
   critical information merely to reduce visible density.
4. For a wizard, justify the split by dependency, complexity or risk. Carry
   inputs forward, support back/edit, summarize consequences and define resume.
   Do not add steps just to create progress screens.
5. Cover applicable loading, empty, partial, validation, blocked permission,
   unavailable dependency, unsaved, success and recovery paths. Keep requested,
   persisted and confirmed states distinct.
6. Validate uncertain grouping/navigation with suitable actual evidence, such
   as task walkthroughs or tree tests. A heuristic inspection can identify risk;
   it does not certify findability or completion rates.

## Deliver one decision-ready map

Carry source-linked findings, the chosen structure, critical paths/states and
remaining decision gaps into the Design handoff. Use extra journey/IA documents
only if the task needs them. Product owns a changed behavior contract; Design
proposes the bounded delta and evidence. Advance to direction/prototype when
the structure is sufficient, keeping residual uncertainty visible.
